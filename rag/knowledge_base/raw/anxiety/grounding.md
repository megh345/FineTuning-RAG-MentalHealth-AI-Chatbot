# Grounding Techniques

## The 5-4-3-2-1 exercise
<!-- chunk_id: grounding_54321_steps -->
Take one slow breath, then notice 5 things you can see, 4 things you can feel, 3 things you can hear, 2 things you can smell, and 1 thing you can taste. This 5-4-3-2-1 exercise redirects attention toward immediate surroundings and the present moment.

## Present-moment grounding for anxiety
<!-- chunk_id: anxiety_grounding_present_moment -->
When thoughts are racing or anxiety feels intense, grounding can help shift attention from distressing thoughts to the present. Place both feet on the floor, notice physical sensations and surroundings, and move through the exercise slowly.

## Grounding does not require perfection
<!-- chunk_id: grounding_optional_not_perfect -->
Grounding is optional and the user does not need to answer every prompt perfectly. The goal is simply to bring attention back to the present. It is not the only response when someone is in immediate danger or expresses self-harm intent.
